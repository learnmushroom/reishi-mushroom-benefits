# Reishi Mushroom (Ganoderma lucidum): Quick Reference

A short, source-linked summary of what research says about reishi.

## What is reishi?
A hard, shelf-like mushroom that grows on decaying hardwood. It is rarely
eaten raw and is usually taken as tea, powder, tincture, or capsule.
Known as "Lingzhi" in Traditional Chinese Medicine.

## Studied benefits (evidence level)

| Area | Evidence | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| Immune support | Small clinical studies | Beta-glucans interact with white blood cells |
| Stress and fatigue | Pilot trials | Small sample sizes |
| Sleep | Limited human data | Mostly traditional use |
| Antioxidant capacity | Small human studies | Raises plasma antioxidant capacity |
| Heart health markers | Mixed human results | Promising, not proven |
| Liver protection | Preclinical only | Lab and animal studies |

Reishi is not a treatment or cure for any disease.

## Common forms
Powder, dual-extracted capsules, tinctures, tea, mushroom coffee.
Typical intake in the source guide: about 1-2 g of dual-extracted
reishi per day. Start low and build slowly.

## Safety checklist
Talk to a doctor first if you:
- take blood thinners or blood-pressure medication
- are pregnant or breastfeeding
- have a bleeding disorder or upcoming surgery
